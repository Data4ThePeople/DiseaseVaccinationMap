By 2015, the share of Utah kindergartners vaccinated against measles had slipped below 95 percent, the level needed to stop the virus from spreading. A decade later, Utah has the highest measles rate in the country.

The warning was public all along, in state reports and federal data tables. So why did so few of the people in a position to act on it see it?

That is the question Dr. Michael Kinch takes on in a guest essay published on our site today. Mike literally wrote the book on vaccines and infectious diseases, and he helped us enhance and validate the data behind our new interactive map of disease and vaccination in every state.

In the essay, Mike makes the case that this is a failure of communication, not of evidence. The numbers live in spreadsheets and technical reports, while vaccine opponents have mastered the forms that actually reach people. Open data can help the people parents trust, like pediatricians, school nurses and local journalists, see the signals before an outbreak rather than after.
