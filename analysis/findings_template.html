When vaccination falls, where does disease follow?

Places where school vaccination coverage dropped and an outbreak came after, plus the real story behind the mumps “wave.”

October 6, 2026 · Step 1 analysis for the disease and vaccination map · Data and code: GitHub

Read this first. These are pairings in time and place, not causes. A drop in coverage followed by an outbreak in the same county is worth reporting, but it does not show that one produced the other. Outbreaks also depend on travel, on who is unvaccinated and how closely they live, and on chance. School coverage is reported by each state in its own way, and most counties have figures only from 2017-18 or 2019-20 on.

Part 1

Where coverage fell and an outbreak followed

The clearest cases are at the county level, and almost all are measles in 2025 and 2026. Each chart shows the share of kindergartners with two MMR doses, from the first school year with a county figure to 2024-25. The dashed line marks 95%.

Coverage: Johns Hopkins county MMR dataset (Dong et al., JAMA 2025), from state school reports; Spartanburg County figures cover all grades, K through 12. Cases: Johns Hopkins Measles Tracking Team, data through September 25, 2026. The map on the main page ends in 2025, so the 2026 cases are not on it.

Largest declineMohave County, Arizona, and southwest Utah

Mohave County's kindergarten MMR coverage fell from 92.1% in 2017-18 to 76.0% in 2023-24, the steepest drop among the outbreak counties, and was 78.4% in 2024-25. The tracker counts 216 measles cases there in 2025 and 69 in 2026. Next door, Washington County, Utah, fell from 88.5% to 79.2%. Utah reports cases by health district: 155 in 2025 and 112 in 2026 in its Southwest district.

Utah DHHS, “2025–2026 Utah measles response” (updated September 29, 2026): 724 Utah residents diagnosed in this outbreak, 197 in 2025 and 527 in 2026, “most” of them unvaccinated. A live page; the count will change.

Still needed: an Arizona or Mohave County source. We have not confirmed whether the Arizona and Utah cases are one outbreak.

Largest outbreakSpartanburg County, South Carolina

Coverage across all grades fell from 95.1% in 2019-20 to 90.0% in 2024-25. The tracker counts 330 cases in 2025 and 610 in 2026, more than any other county.

SC Department of Public Health, “DPH Confirms Measles Outbreak in Upstate Region” (October 2, 2025).

SC Department of Public Health, “DPH Announces End to Measles Outbreak in Upstate at 997 Cases” (April 27, 2026): 997 cases from October 2025 through March 2026, 932 of them in unvaccinated people.

Low for yearsGaines County, Texas

Gaines County is a different kind of story. Coverage was already 80.4% in 2019-20, stayed between 80% and 83%, and was 77.3% in 2024-25, far below the Texas county median of 94.5%. The tracker counts 414 cases in 2025. This is a pocket that stayed low, not a recent decline, and that may be the more accurate frame for how outbreaks start.

CDC, “Notes from the Field: Initial Public Health Response to a Measles Outbreak in a Close-Knit West Texas Community”: 207 cases from January 29 to February 28, 2025.

Texas DSHS, “Texas announces end of West Texas measles outbreak” (August 18, 2025): 762 confirmed cases.

Background: Texas House Research Organization, “Conscientious Objection to Immunization” (2003), on the law that created the state's exemption.

2026Lancaster County, Pennsylvania, and Collier County, Florida

Lancaster fell from 95.5% in 2017-18 to 88.5% in 2024-25 and had 359 cases in 2026. Collier fell from 93.8% to 86.3% and had 99 cases in 2026. In September 2025 Florida's surgeon general announced plans to end childhood vaccine mandates (WUSF/AP); Collier's decline began years earlier.

Still needed: contemporary sources for both 2026 outbreaks.

Clearest statewide declineIdaho

The share of Idaho kindergartners with a non-medical exemption rose from 3.5% in 2009-10 to 17.0% in 2025-26, the highest of any state. Kindergarten MMR coverage fell from 87.0% to 75.2% over the same years. Whooping cough reached 59.9 cases per 100,000 people in 2024, 417% above Idaho's own 2015 to 2019 average; the national rate rose 126% on the same comparison. Measles returned in 2023.

Idaho DHW, “Measles is back in Idaho” (October 6, 2023): 10 cases in southwest Idaho, after two in the state in the prior 20 years.

Idaho Legislature, Senate Bill 1210, the Idaho Medical Freedom Act (signed April 4, 2025).

Still needed: an Idaho source on the 2024 whooping cough rise.

What the data does not show

  • A strong statewide link for measles. Across 46 states, a bigger drop in kindergarten MMR coverage from 2019-20 to 2024-25 goes with a higher 2025 measles rate, but only moderately (rank correlation −0.45). Most of the 2025 total sits in a handful of counties.
  • A statewide link for whooping cough. Cases rose from 7,063 in 2023 to 43,321 in 2024 across the country. Idaho and Wisconsin, with large DTaP drops, rose sharply, but so did states with almost no drop: Rhode Island (324% above its own 2015 to 2019 average), Connecticut (339%) and New York (241%). The rank correlation is weak (−0.24).
  • Coverage before the earlier outbreaks. Minnesota 2017, Clark County 2019 and New York 2019 came before most county figures begin.

Part 2

The mumps “wave” is mostly the start of the record

On the map, mumps appears in 1968 at its highest level and then shrinks. That is when it was first counted state by state, not when an epidemic began. Then come the vaccine era and a resurgence in 1987 that CDC tied to a gap in vaccination at the time.

Line: reported mumps cases, summed over the states reporting each year (Project Tycho through 1992, CDC final annual tables from 1993). Circles: national totals CDC printed in its 1989 mumps recommendations. The shaded years rest on provisional reports that undercount (1986 shows 2,676 against CDC's 7,790) and are being re-extracted from CDC's scanned annual reports.

Before 1968Mumps was common long before the map starts

About 110 large cities reported mumps from 1924 to 1932, between 24,585 and 47,208 cases a year in those cities alone, before city reporting of mumps stops in the data. CDC counted 185,691 cases nationwide in 1967.

1967 to 1985The decline lines up with the vaccine

The mumps vaccine was licensed in 1967 and recommended for routine use in 1977. CDC reported a 98% decline, from 185,691 cases in 1967 to 2,982 in 1985.

1986 to 1987The resurgence, and why CDC said it happened

Reported cases rose to 12,848 in 1987. The steepest increases were among 10 to 14 year olds (almost 600%) and 15 to 19 year olds (more than 700%). CDC attributed the shift to “the relatively underimmunized cohort of children born between 1967 and 1977”: born after the vaccine existed, but before it was routinely given. CDC also compared states. In 1987 the 14 states with no school requirement for mumps vaccine had 11.5 cases per 100,000 people, against 1.1 in the 15 areas that required it from kindergarten through 12th grade. Illinois (23.2 per 100,000), Wisconsin (35.3) and Tennessee (24.6) led that year in our data.

CDC, “Recommendations of the Immunization Practices Advisory Committee Mumps Prevention” (June 9, 1989).

CDC, “Mumps Outbreaks on University Campuses -- Illinois, Wisconsin, South Dakota” (1987).

This is the one case in the record where CDC itself tied a gap in vaccination to a rise in disease at the time, which makes it the cleanest fit for the question.

2006 and 2016 to 2017Later outbreaks are a different story

The 2006 outbreak (6,584 cases, starting in Iowa) and the 2016 and 2017 outbreaks (6,369 and 6,109 cases, in Arkansas, Hawaii, Washington and on several campuses) happened largely among people who had been vaccinated. In January 2018 CDC's advisory committee recommended a third dose for people at higher risk during an outbreak. These are about protection fading in close-contact settings, not about falling coverage.

CDC, “Mumps Epidemic --- Iowa, 2006”; third-dose recommendation (January 2018).
